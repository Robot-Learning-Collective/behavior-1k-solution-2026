## EVAL PROCEDURE
**Partial learned procedure, not a solved task.** Evaluation starts in a **fresh evaluator world**, using only its authorized public RGBD + robot self-state and its matching control interface. Never bind to the practice worker/client/queue below, reuse old coordinates as targets, or call practice reset/restore/feedback. Historical predicate feedback, snapshots, paths and instance geometry below are training evidence, not runtime policy inputs.

1. Observe the fresh kitchen; identify refrigerator, sprouts/container, knife, board, baking sheet and oven visually. Measure current accessible surfaces/handles with RGBD. Use measured joints/limits and inspected free-space approaches; endpoint IK is not swept collision clearance.
2. Open refrigerator sufficiently for extraction. Adapt handle arc or accessible inner-face pushes from current door geometry; release preload rather than force a fixed-wrist pull. Acquire the loaded container rim, verify lift **and carry**, then withdraw clear of shelf/door. Close the fridge once extraction clearance allows. [Retrieval example](examples/fridge-container-001.md) is one training instance, not a portable trajectory; refrigerator closure is not yet practised successfully.
3. Prepare workspace: board and sheet adjacent, container outside arm/food approach corridors. Retain a proven container grasp through gradual, inspected orientation changes to decant; verify actual food count/location and empty-container release. Large Cartesian pour rotations diverged; staged joint-space orientation changes worked once. [Decant/transfer](examples/decant-transfer-002.md). For the empty sheet, identify an accessible short rim and its local normal in fresh RGBD. Stage above it, orient fingers modestly forward/down and close across the rim; check low-pose reach as well as high staging before descent. One such grasp retained the empty sheet through lift AND lateral carry. Lift enough to clear intervening bowl/food, place over measured clear steel beside the board, release persistent preload on support, open, then withdraw and verify the sheet stays flat. Do not infer retention from asymmetric aperture or placement from reached TCP. [Tray study](examples/tray-human-study-004.md), [own evidence and failures](examples/tray-placement-004.md). This is one empty-sheet success, NOT loaded-sheet handling or independent repeatability; it postdates the original frozen capture.
4. Grasp knife by its visible handle; verify retained lift/carry. Reorient in free space from the **actual blade/edge**, not an assumed wrist axis. Manipulate whole sprouts with the other hand: stage XY above the fresh target, then lower to side/lower-body contact. An oblique forward/down finger approach with lateral closure succeeded after fore-aft/vertical approaches rolled food or hit bowl. Verify retained lift/carry and release/withdrawal; remeasure any rolling food. [Final pickup adaptation and failures](examples/four-cuts-003.md).
5. Place each whole on a clear board area and withdraw the free hand. Align the actual cutting edge using visible blade geometry and current depth. A modest tip-down orientation gave gripper clearance in practice. Use a bounded downward pass, inspect the physical split, release persistent preload with valid measured-joint targets, lift and inspect halves; a stalled endpoint can follow a successful split. Do not infer success from `achieved`, aperture or effort. Four training sprouts were cut: first needed two approaches; later three each one downward pass (second sprout used two depth increments). [Cutting detail](examples/knife-cutting-002.md), [all four cuts](examples/four-cuts-003.md).
6. Put knife on empty support, open and withdraw; verify it remains. Transfer **all eight** halves to adjacent sheet, checking each after release/withdrawal rather than counting attempts. Three public human episodes informed this sequence and oblique approach; they were sampled with aligned measured state **and requested actions**, not blindly replayed. [Human comparison](examples/human-three-episode-study.md). One half was retained through lift/carry and remained inside the sheet after release/withdrawal; seven remain. Its Cartesian withdrawal diverged despite low joint lag, then an inspected explicit joint return recovered. A subsequent crowded-row approach rolled another half onto steel; a board-edge pinch closed empty on lift. [Ongoing half evidence/failures](examples/half-transfer-005.md). These are not eight-half transfer mastery.
7. Open oven, acquire loaded sheet with stable support, insert it fully without clipping rack/door, release/withdraw, activate cooking as the observed appliance requires, and close oven. Final goal: all eight halves cooked and inside oven, both oven and refrigerator closed. **Loaded-sheet handling, oven insertion/activation, cooking and both-door completion are unlearned; adapt from current observations, do not invent a proven recipe.** Evaluation scoring belongs to the evaluator; no training-feedback calls.

Known failures: shallow rim grasps slipped after transient lift; the bowl was accidentally grasped instead of food; exact-axis poses and large rotations saturated joints; diagonal descents rolled food; folding near table hit its edge; one guarded base retreat was not retried. Cancel persistent arm preload using explicitly valid measured joints with adequate slew, inspect native safety stops, and never replay an uncertain action. No whole-task success, independent repeatability or held-out result. Evidence is one TRAIN10 practice trajectory with same-source restores plus public demonstrations; four-cut training feedback verified eight real halves, none cooked/in oven. Existing numerical examples are diagnostic records, not collision-safe macros.
