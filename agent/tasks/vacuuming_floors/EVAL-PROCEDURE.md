1. Locate upright vacuum and visible dirt using all cameras. Use native float-depth BODY points to align base with an accessible grasp region. Clear both arms before crouching; stage a bent elbow in clear space before large Cartesian orientation changes, since straight-arm initialization can select a poor IK branch. If the free elbow blocks while tucking inward, clear it outward first. Intermediate crouch worked for motor grasps; normal torso worked for a higher shaft grasp. Neither posture guarantees clearance.
2. Prefer a measured accessible grasp, not a fixed motor-top coordinate. Lower motor-body grasp with near-down fingers/lateral jaws worked on20/188 but closed empty twice on256. After an empty closure, withdraw before reorienting; inspect pad/body alignment and reach margin. A tested fallback is a narrow exposed shaft segment BETWEEN motor and switch: orient fingers across it, jaws spanning its width, approach open and close separately. Leave substantial free-hand/wrist clearance below the switch; a grasp too near the button can retain yet obstruct activation. Inspect closure prerequisites—pending base/arm goals may reject closure without motion; release/anchor their actual endpoints rather than replay travel. Verify retention by visible nozzle/tool movement on a bounded lift; symmetric or asymmetric aperture alone is not proof. See `examples/shaft-grasp-and-clearance.md`.
