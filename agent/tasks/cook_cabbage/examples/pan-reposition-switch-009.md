# Loaded-pan repositioning and stove activation — one training sequence

Public RGBD/self-state evidence, corrected sourcea32648a, TRAIN10, obs135–162. The earlier two-episode switch study is [008](human-switch-comparison-008.md). This is one adapted sequence, with failures preserved—not a replay script or repeatability claim. All coordinates below are historical BODY metres and must be remeasured.

## Reusable procedure
1. Preserve front-control access when placing the pan. A visible red marker can be rendered through an obstructing bowl; image visibility is not a contact route. Compare rim, bowl floor and control depths. Here the initial pan placement blocked descent, unlike the two human references.
2. If relocation is necessary, prefer the accessible handle. A front-right rim pinch worked once here: open near-vertical jaws outside the rim, lower with the lower pad clear of counter, insert above/below the lip, close, then verify retained lift. Closing itself tilted the loaded pan about14°; do not assume an edge grasp preserves level.
3. With support clearance, fit a plane to verified empty flat-bottom pixels, check a fourth point, and rotate the measured gripper frame by the minimal rotation taking that normal to BODY+Z. Recheck the actual pan, not just commanded orientation. Keep contents inside during transport; avoid high acceleration and marginal reach.
4. The long arm-only loaded reach hit near-straight elbow plus wrist lag. Release measured-q preload, retract to a reachable branch, relevel, then adapt a torso-assisted placement. For this geometry, matched opposite torso-joint increments preserved chest pitch and produced a monotonic FK descent; check the entire slew and all links/objects, not just endpoints. On support, replace preload, open, withdraw jaws out of the rim and verify the pan remains.
5. Close empty fingers separately if combined pose+close reports `closing_deferred`. Orient fingertips forward/down, with closure across the control. Approach from in FRONT of the pan lip, lower outside it, then advance below the overhang toward the measured control. A top-down approach can hit the rim even when the button is not under the pan floor.
6. Inspect the actual red→green change. Reaching a nearby target, overlap in the wrist image, or settling alone is insufficient. Once green, replace preload and withdraw without continuing to press. A direct Cartesian retraction here dipped into the counter region; measured-q replacement followed by a clear-lane base retreat succeeded. Do not replay the failed arm withdrawal.
7. Inspect pan support and material change after clearing the hand. Official evaluation—not a practice-feedback call—determines completion in a fresh evaluator world. This sequence produced a green control and visibly changed material; the separate operator-only training result records native success. No privileged feedback, snapshot or practice binding belongs in evaluator policy input.
